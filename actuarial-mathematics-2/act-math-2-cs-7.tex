What are the methods required by law for the actuarial reserve?

For life insurance products, the future benefits are usually supported by preceeding
premium payments. This time difference of cash in/outflow introduces the necessity of an account management.
There are two methods for determining the required reserve
the prospective approach:
present value of future benefits = prospective reserve + present value of future premiums
the retrospective approach:
terminal value of previous premiums = retrospective reserve + terminal value of previous benefits

According to German HGB the prospective approach should always be preferred. The retrospective method
is especially useful under the assumption of stationarity of the distribution (past performance predicts future value).


Give formula's for the actuarial reserve 

Assumptions: \\
Payment times: \( \{0 = s_0, s_1, \dots, s_k \}\) \\
Death benefit: \( D(s_{k+1}) \) if death occurs in \( (s_k, s_{k+1}] \), payable in \( s_{k+1} \) \\
Survival benefit: \( S(s_k) \) if surviving until \( s_k \) , payable in \( s_k \) \\
Premium \( P_x(s_k) \) for \( x \)-year old insured person in period \( (s_k , s_{k+1}] \), payable in advance in \( s_k \) \\
Calculatory maximum age \( w_x \)  \\

Assume \( s_k = k \) then the reserve can be expressed as: 
\[ 
\Vx[k]{x} = \sum_{l=k}^{w - x} D(l + 1)v^{l + 1 - k} \px[l - k]{x + k} \cdot \qx{x + l} + \sum_{l = k}^{w - x} (S(l) - P_x(l)) v^{l - k} \px[l - k]{x + k}
\]

For inter-annual time points \( k + u \) with \( u \in [0, 1] \) under the linearity assumption \( \qx[u]{x} = u \qx{x} \)
we get instead
\[ \Vx[k + u]{x} = \frac{v^{1-u}}{1 - u\qx{x+k}} (D(k+1)(1 - u)\qx{x+k} + \Vx[k+1]{x} \px{x+k}) \]

Example \( n \)-year insurance for survival with insured sum \( 1 \) and constant annual premiums
\( P_{x,n}(\Ex[n]{x}) \) due in advance:
\[ \Vx[k]{x}(\Ex[n]{x}) = \Ex[n-k]{x+k} - P_{x,n}(\Ex[n]{x})\ax**{x+k: \angl{n - k}} = \frac{D_{x+n}}{D_{x+k}}(1 - \frac{N_{x+k} - N_{x+n}}{N_x - N_{x+n}}) \]

The actuarial balance equation or fundamental equation is a recursive expression for \( \Vx[k]{x} \) which allows to fast track some computations: \\
Forward recursion (prospective):
\[ \Vx[k+1]{x} = \frac{1}{v \px{x+k}}(\Vx[k]{x} - S(k) + P_x(k) - v\qx{x+k} D(k+1)) \]
with \( \Vx[0]{x} = 0 \). \\
Backward recursion (retrospective):
\[ \Vx[k]{x} = S(k) - P_x(k) + v \qx{x+k} D(k+1) + v\px{x+k} \Vx[k+1]{x} \]
with \( \Vx[n]{x} = S(n) \) or \( 0 \) if no final survival benefit is included. \\

This also yields an expression for the premia in terms of the reserve :
\[ P_x(k) = v \Vx[k+1]{x} - \Vx[k]{x} + v(D(k+1) - \Vx{k+1}{x})\qx{x+k}\]
The premia can be decomposed into a savings and risk component which are expressed in terms of the reserve:
\[ P^S_x(k) = v  \Vx[k+1]{x} - \Vx[k]{x} \]
The savings component is for the adjustment of the prospetive reserve at the end of the year.
Typically it turns negative over the insurance term. 
\[ P^R_x(k) = v(D(k+1) - \Vx{k+1}{x})\qx{x+k} \]
The risk component is for covering the risk (of death) in the period \( (k, k+1] \) and it corresponds
to the premium of a term life insurance in \( (k, k+1] \) with insured sum \( D(k+1) - \Vx[k+1]{x} \).
Typically it increases over the insurance term.


Explain how to determine the reserve heuristically

Originally the reserve is defined as the difference between benefits and premia, either under the retro- or prospective approach.

If we let \( \actsymb[m][L]{B}{x}[\text{pro}] \) denote the future benefits of a contract for an individual aged \( x \) 
discounted to time point \( m \) and \( \actsymb[m][P]{B}{x}[\text{pro}] \) the remaining premia discounted, then the reserve is
\[ \Vx[m]{x} = \mathbb{E}[\actsymb[m][L]{B}{x}[\text{pro}]] - \mathbb{E}[\actsymb[m][P]{B}{x}[\text{pro}]] \]
The condition \(\Vx[0]{x} = 0\) forces \( \mathbb{E}[\actsymb[0][L]{B}{x}[\text{pro}]] = \mathbb{E}[\actsymb[0][P]{B}{x}[\text{pro}]] \),
making it clear that premia overtime are equivalent to the future benefits.
The corresponding retrospective definition instead uses the past benefits and premia with accrued interest until \( m \):
\[ \Vx[m]{x} = \mathbb{E}[\actsymb[m][L]{B}{x}[\text{ret}]] - \mathbb{E}[\actsymb[m][P]{B}{x}[\text{ret}]] \]
\\
With this more general definition its usually faster for standard products to determine the reserve than with recursion.

Consider an \( n \)-term endowement insurance with insured sum \( 1 \) and recurring payments in advance:
\[
  \actsymb[m][L]{B}{x}[\text{pro}]  = v^{n-m} \mathbf{1}_{T_x > m}
\]
Let \( \Px[0]{x} = \frac{\Ex[n]{x}}{\ax**{x:\angl{n}}} \) then
\[
  \actsymb[m][P]{B}{x}[\text{pro}] = \Px[0]{x} \sum_{k=0}^{n - m - 1} v^k \mathbf{1}_{T_x > m + k}
\]
thus the reserve is
\begin{align*}
  \Vx[m]{x} &= v^{n - m} \px[n-m]{x + m} - \Px[0]{x} \sum_{k=0}^{n - m - 1} v^k \px[k]{x + m} \\
            &= \Ex[x + m]{n - m} -  \Px[0]{x} \ax**{x + m : \angl{n - m}} \\
\end{align*}
The general pattern is for premium payment until \( t \leq n \): \\
\[\Vx[m]{x} = L - P \]
where 
\[ L = \text{remaining benefits for } n - m \text{ discounted to } m \]
\[ P = \text{premia discounted for remaining payment term } t - m \]
both considered for an individual aged \( x + m \).


Define the Zillmerized and sufficient reserve

The Zillmerized reserve \( \Vx[m]{x}[Z] \) includes the direct acquisition cost into the premia,
that is for payment term \( t \leq n \) and contract length \( n \) 
\[ 
  P^Z_x = \frac{ \mathbb{E}[\actsymb[L][0]{B}{x}] }{ \ax**{x:\angl t} - \alpha^Z t}
\]
yielding for \( m < t \):
\[ 
  \Vx[m]{x}[Z] = \mathbb{E}[\actsymb[m][L]{B}{x} ] - P^Z_x \ax**{x+m:\angl{t - m}}
\]
and for \( m \geq t \)
\[ 
  \Vx[m]{x}[Z] = \mathbb{E}[ \actsymb[m][L]{B}{x} ]
\]
Since it always holds that \( P^Z_x > P_x \) we have \( \Vx[m]{x}[Z] \leq \Vx[m]{x} \), which is strict for \( m < t \)
and an equality for \( m \geq t \). As \( \Vx[m]{x} = 0 \), the Zillmerized reserve starts out negative.

The sufficient reserve \( \Vx[m][a]{x} \) is for \( m < t \):
\[
  \Vx[m][a]{x} = \mathbb{E}[ \actsymb[m][L]{B}{x} ] + ( \alpha^\gamma \text{GA}^\gamma + \gamma_1 GG)\cdot \ax**{x+m:\angl{t - m}}
  + \gamma_2 \text{GG} \cdot \ax**[t - m]{x+m:\angl{n - t}} - (1 - \beta) \Px[][a]{x} \cdot \ax**{x+m:\angl{t - m}}
\]
and for \( t > m \):
\[
  \Vx[m][a]{x} = \mathbb{E}[ \actsymb[m][L]{B}{x} ] + \gamma_2 \text{GG} \cdot \ax**{x+m:\angl{n - m}}
\]
where \( \Px[][a]{x} \) is the sufficient premia (including all costs).
