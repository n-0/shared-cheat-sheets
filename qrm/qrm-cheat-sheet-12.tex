What is risk sharing among entities? How is the total SCR computed for a single
aggregate entity in comparison to several individually regulated entities?

Financial firm is not consolidated, but forms a corporate network:

- Subentities \(i=1,2, \dots, n\),
- Individually regulated according to risk measures \(\rho^i\)

The total balance sheet can be split among the sub-entities using legally binding transfer agreements:
\[(E_t^i)_{i=1,2, \dots, n} \text{ with } E_t = \sum_{i=1}^n E_t^i, \quad t=0,1\]

This implies that the aggregate SCR of the network becomes
\[\sum_{i=1}^n SCR^i = E_0 + \sum_{i=1}^n \rho^i(E^i).\]

- The corporate network can design optimal transfer agreements in order to minimize the SCR, 
  leading to the following \textbf{optimal risk sharing problem}:
\[\Box_{i=1}^n \rho^i (E) := \inf \left\{ \sum_{i=1}^n \rho^i(E^i) : \sum_{i=1}^n E^i = E, E^1, E^2, \dots, E^n \in \mathcal{X} \right\}\]
- If \(\rho^i = \rho \quad \forall i\) is coherent, then \(\rho(E) \leq \sum_{i=1}^n \rho^i(E^i)\) and it is optimal to allocate the 
  total net asset value to one entity, e.g. the holding company.
- For non-coherent risk measures, the inequality may be reversed: 
  Decentralized risk measurement and appropriate transfer agreements between multiple entities might sweep the downside risk under the carpet.

What problems arise in the context of V@R-based risk
measures for sharing risk among companies?

Definition
A distribution-based risk measure \(\rho: L^{\infty} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}\) is a V@R-type risk measure with parameter \(\alpha > 0\), if
\[\rho(X) = E \left[ X \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\{-V@R_{\alpha}(X) \leq X\}} - V@R_{\alpha}(X) \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\{-V@R_{\alpha}(X) > X\}} \right] \quad (X \in L^{\infty}).\]

Theorem
Let \(E \in L^{\infty}, \rho^1, \rho^2, \dots, \rho^n\) be normalized V@R-type risk measures with parameters \(\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \dots, \alpha_n\), and assume that the allocation \((E^i)_{i=1,2,\dots,n}\) is constructed as before. If \(d = \sum_{i=1}^n \alpha_i \geq 1\), then
\[\Box_{i=1}^n \rho^i(E) \leq \sum_{i=1}^n \rho^i(E^i) = -\text{ess sup } E.\]

Worst case scenario is when one of the \( \rho^i \) is also strongly surplus sensitive at level \( α > 0 \)
because then 
\[\Box_{i=1}^n \rho^i(E) = - \infty.\]

What is a set-valued systemic risk measure? State elementary properties. How are
cash-invariant allocation rules related to regulatory prices of capital for different
entities?

Systemic risk is measured by the set of allocations of additional capital that lead to acceptable outcomes.

Letting \(\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^n; \mathbb{R}_+) := \{ B \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n \mid B = B + \mathbb{R}^n_+ \}\) 
be the collection of upper sets with ordering cone \(\mathbb{R}_+\), we call the function 

\(R: \mathcal{Y} \times \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^n; \mathbb{R}_+^n)\)
a systemic risk measure, if for some acceptance set \(\mathcal{A} \subseteq \mathcal{X}\) of a scalar monetary risk measure:

\[ R(Y; k) = \{ m \in \mathbb{R}^n \mid Y_{k+m} \in \mathcal{A} \}. \]

Elementary Properties:
1. Cash-invariance:  \( R(Y; k) + m = R(Y; k - m) \)
2. Monotonicity:  \( Y \geq Z \implies (\forall k \in \mathbb{R}^n: R(Y; k) \supseteq R(Z; k)) \)
